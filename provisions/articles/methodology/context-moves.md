# Context moves

Every piece of context has a purpose and a place. Four moves cover it.

- **Make.** Produce it for a job that needs it.
- **Retain.** Keep it while that purpose still needs it, in the one home that owns it.
- **Surface.** Bring it back when it is needed. A turn that does not need it does not see it.
- **Discard.** Drop it when the purpose is over.

A pile with no reconcile grows until nobody trusts it. Reconcile on a cadence, or when asked. One pass. Each item stays where it belongs, gets surfaced, or is discarded. An item does not move to a longer life because a tool was helpful.

Where a system has named levels, those levels are the scale. Record them in one place. Do not keep a second scale beside them.

Relation to `methodology/canonical-home`: retaining means one home.

Relation to `methodology/nexus-board`: that article is the ranked week and the session-start pack. This one is lifetime and attention. The weekly pack walks whatever levels exist. It does not replace the board.

**Executable.** Skill `weekly-pack`, on a chair that maps it. The skill is the reconcile pass. A chair without the skill still uses the four moves.

**Class: composition, opportunistic.** The four moves assume no particular store. The skill and any named levels are present only where a chair has them.

Reason: context is cheap to make and expensive to keep in the wrong place. An agent that retains everything, or shows it on every turn, spends the person's attention on piles. The reconcile is what keeps a level from becoming a landfill.
